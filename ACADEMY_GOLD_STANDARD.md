# Wealth Lab Academy — Gold Standard Textbook Quality Contract

**Effective:** 2026-10-31 · **First pilot:** L1.S1 Edition 2.0 · **Status:** This is the mandatory depth target for future *revisions of existing lessons*. It does not mean other lessons have already reached this level.

## Mission

Move Wealth Lab from short explainers and isolated quizzes to substantive, accessible **university-style self-study**. Keep explanations simple enough for beginners to follow, but progressively introduce professional accounting, investing, finance, legal and risk-analysis reasoning.

This remains an independent learning product: it is **not an accredited college, degree program, CFP Board Registered Program, CFA curriculum, or professional credential**. Those qualifications require third-party authorization.

## Required chapter progression

A complete chapter should support all of the following:

1. **Objectives and prerequisite concepts.** State what learners will understand, calculate, explain and decide independently; introduce new terms only after a concrete explanation.
2. **Teaching before testing.** Start with plain-English mental models, then introduce the formal vocabulary, mathematical mechanics, and limitations. Do not treat one formula and three paragraphs as complete instruction.
3. **Worked mathematics with every step.** Show inputs, dates, units, formula, substitutions, arithmetic, interpretation and alternate scenarios. Explain why the formula is valid and when it is not applicable.
4. **Economic intuition.** Describe how a household, lender, insurer, issuer or investor experiences the result in real life; distinguish accounting changes from cash payments and risks.
5. **Risk and failure analysis.** Include plausible negative outcomes, hidden costs, assumption sensitivities, contradictory incentives and situations where the attractive strategy is unsuitable.
6. **Decision-making in context.** Compare at least two genuine alternatives using the same dates and definitions. Include what evidence or professional expertise would be needed to make the decision responsibly.
7. **Embedded terminology.** Explain terms near first use and maintain a review glossary, without flooding a phone screen with unexplained jargon.
8. **Independent learner work.** Require computations, short explanations, applied cases and feedback—not only recall-based multiple choice questions. A self-check worksheet complements but does not replace assessed work.
9. **Evidence and freshness.** Link to authoritative original educational or regulatory sources. Date-sensitive rates, eligibility, tax thresholds and laws must be verified for the applicable date, location and person; hypothetical rates must be labeled as such.
10. **Accessible reading.** Use mobile-sized reading parts, meaningful heading hierarchy, expandable supporting material, clear tables, good contrast, and the approved Deep Emerald / Rich Gold reader system.
11. **Backward compatibility.** Maintain existing stable L*.S* section IDs, notes, bookmarks and stored learning data. Do not overwrite or reorder previous part-question IDs or final-question IDs. New assessments may affect *current edition mastery eligibility* but may not erase previous answers or completion records.
12. **Technical verification.** Reject publication if content registers incorrectly, has unsupported financial claims, invalid arithmetic, missing sources, layout overflow, or broken offline assets.

## Depth and assessment targets

- A complex finance chapter normally needs **approximately 4,000–8,000 explanatory words**, sometimes more and sometimes less. This is a *quality range*, not padding to reach a count.
- Break a long chapter into approximately **8–12 substantial mobile reading parts**. Each part should explain principles, give a worked example, identify a misleading interpretation, and assign independent practice.
- For technical calculations include at least two distinct cases—e.g. expected versus adverse outcome—with all assumptions recorded.
- Add **2 or more formative checks per reading part**, plus a cumulative assessment and a realistic multi-step professional case.
- Include an interactive worksheet/calculator where numerical fluency matters; ask for an independent calculation before revealing reasoning.
- A completed checkbox is not proof of professional competency. Existing 80% overall / 75% chapter-mastery threshold is only a *self-study signal*. Comprehensive academic assessment would also require reviewed written work, extensive calculations, project rubrics, and external institutional standards.

## First gold-standard pilot: L1.S1 — The Wealth Equation

Edition 2.0 extends the **six old reading parts in place** and appends six more. Existing part indices and corresponding stored answer keys for parts 1–6 remain aligned. Original final questions remain in original positions; added questions use new indices. Learners retain existing notes/bookmarks and completion flags; new questions must be answered to meet the expanded edition's full mastery threshold.

Original topics deepened:
- Income, household cash flow, personal balance sheet and net worth.
- Asset values, debt, unsecured borrowing capacity, collateral and negative equity.
- Down payment versus closing costs and home-equity risk.
- Bill timing, liquidity, reserves and cash runway.
- Principal, interest, transfers and the net-worth reconciliation.
- Inheritance as an economic plan and not only a transfer of title.

Six **new** reading parts:
- Build and reconcile the personal balance sheet using dated evidence.
- Map two-paycheck cash flow by transaction date.
- Reconcile loan principal, interest, borrowing and transfers.
- Stress test home equity, price shocks and selling expenses.
- Separate scheduled bills, emergency reserve and flexible cash.
- Produce a professional decision memorandum with multiple scenarios.

Added Features:
- Private on-screen balance-sheet and cash-availability worksheet inside **Examples**; user inputs are **not persisted**.
- Three numerical free-entry self-checks with explanation of each answer, in addition to existing four-choice assessments.
- A mortgage case with multiple arithmetic steps and a downside scenario, including hypothetical selling costs.
- Formal text-based regression to check instructional depth, numeric examples, original question order and safe offline loading.
